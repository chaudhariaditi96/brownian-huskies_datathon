# Hypothesis Card

## Title
Investigating the cellular origins of altered angiogenic gene expression in early-onset preeclampsia
## Clinical/Biological Problem
Preeclampsia is a pregnancy complication associated with hypertension, placental dysfunction, and increased maternal and fetal risk. Angiogenic imbalance is an important feature of PE, particularly the balance between soluble FMS-like tyrosine kinase-1 (sFlt-1) and placental growth factor (PIGF) (Burwick & Rodriguez, 2024). Previous single-cell transcriptomic studies have identified differences in trophoblast populations and other placental cell types in PE (Admanti et al., 2023; Xiao et al., 2022). However, differences in bulk placental tissue may reflect changes in cell-type proportions rather than changes in gene expression within individual populations (Campbell et al., 2023). Our project investigates these possibilities by examining differentially expressed genes and their association with placental cell populations.

## Why the Result Is Biologically Plausible

Syncytiotrophoblasts play a key role in placental function and maternal-fetal exchange. Recent single-nucleus multi-omic research shows this tissue harbors distinct transcriptional states that shift during pregnancy (Wang et al., 2024). FTL-1 is particularly relevant to PE because its soluble isoform, sFlt-1, contributes to angiogenic imbalance by reducing VEGF and PIGF availability (Burswick & Rodriguez, 2024). Single-cell transcriptomics studies have also identified altered placental gene expression and trophoblast function in PE (Admati et al., 2023; Xiao et al., 2022). In our analysis, ENG, FLT1, LEP, and INHBA were consistently higher in bulk PE placentas, and their expression changes followed the same direction within syncytiotrophoblasts. These findings support further investigation of this population, although they do not establish a single specific mechanism.
